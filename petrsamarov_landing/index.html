Then those systems begin to fail.

Book I · Published November 2026

Voice of the Limit

For thousands of years, the Empire of the Limit has endured. Its institutions are stable. Its rituals are precise. Its people know their place within the Form.

Memory preserves what has been. The Measure defines what should be. And the Will quietly connects every citizen to a civilization that has long since forgotten how much of its own past it no longer understands.

But something is beginning to sound where there should be silence.

Seraphia, a young woman trained to listen to Memory, hears what others cannot. Lucan, an engineer who trusts systems more than metaphysics, encounters an anomaly his instruments cannot explain, one that threatens the life he shares with his husband. Elsar, a meticulous Recorder, watches the certainty of his institutional world begin to fracture. Aivar, a mediator searching through the Empire’s history, discovers patterns hidden behind official explanations. And Sariela Welt, heir to one of the Great Houses, knows that some of those anomalies may not be accidents at all.

Across the Empire, their experiences raise a question its institutions were never designed to confront:

What happens when a civilization preserves its Form long after it has forgotten its meaning?

Voice of the Limit is a philosophical science-fiction novel about memory, identity, power, and the systems people inherit—and what happens when they begin to question whether preservation and survival are really the same thing.

It is the first novel in the Empire of the Limit series.

The Cycle

Three trilogies.
Nine novels.
One world

What holds a civilization together? Can a system ever measure the true value of a human being? And why do we build institutions we hope will outlive us?

Across three trilogies, Empire of the Limit explores these questions through the lives of people whose world is changing faster than their understanding of it.

I

Crisis of the Limit

The system that has preserved memory and sustained the Empire for centuries begins to return contradictory answers. The first trilogy follows the collapse of universal order, the price of preserving it, and the search for another way to connect human beings.

II

Crisis of Measure

A future society attempts to build a system of absolute meritocracy and objective human evaluation. The more precise the system becomes, the more clearly its own limits appear.

III

Crisis of the Human Limit

The final trilogy turns to the question beneath every earlier crisis: why do people build systems designed to survive them, and what remains when no universal order is guaranteed?

Author

Petr Samarov

Petr Samarov is a science fiction author based in Germany. His work explores humanity, memory, identity, and the systems people create to make sense of the world.

His fiction is rooted in a humanistic perspective. Through imagined civilizations and morally complex characters, he examines questions of freedom, responsibility, and the fragile connections that hold people together. His worlds embrace diverse identities and relationships as an ordinary part of human experience.

Alongside his writing, Samarov works in organizational design, exploring how complex structures emerge, evolve, and influence human behavior. This perspective informs his fiction, where institutions and individual choices become part of larger philosophical questions.

He is the author of Voice of the Limit, the first novel in the Empire of the Limit series, and the host of Queer Sci-Fi & Merlot, an independent literary video podcast exploring queer speculative fiction.

He writes in Russian and publishes his work in English translation.

Queer Sci-Fi & Merlot

A literary bar on the edge of the galaxy

Queer Sci-Fi & Merlot is an independent literary video podcast hosted by science fiction author Petr Samarov. The project explores queer science fiction, fantasy, horror, and other forms of speculative fiction through honest book reviews, recommendations, previews of upcoming releases, and conversations about strange worlds and fascinating characters.

With a special place reserved for indie authors and self-published books, the podcast celebrates everything from philosophical space operas and experimental fiction to unapologetically spicy queer romances and old-fashioned adventure. It's a place for thoughtful literary conversations, a sense of humour, and a good glass of wine, without literary snobbery or purity tests.
